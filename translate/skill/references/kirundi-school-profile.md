# Kirundi school-to-family profile

Use for messages from a school, teacher or program to a Kirundi-speaking parent or guardian.

## Reader and register

- The reader is an adult family member, often newer to the school system. Write the way a
  respectful school employee speaks to a parent: polite, direct, everyday words.
- "Casual" means clear and friendly. It never weakens a concern or a requirement.
- Do not use religious, proverbial or old-fashioned phrasing. Some Kirundi reference books lean
  that way; school letters do not.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence where the English allows it.

## Keep the meaning exactly

- Who did or said something, and who is asked to respond.
- What the school saw itself, as against what someone reported or suspects.
- "Not", "never", "no longer".
- How often: sometimes stays sometimes, never becomes always.
- May, can, should, must: keep the same strength.
- How urgent it is, and any deadline.
- Do not add a diagnosis, blame, a promise, a policy or a consequence.

## Names and school terms

- People's names, school names, program names, app names: copy unchanged.
- A school term with no sure Kirundi word (for example IEP, PowerSchool, a named reading program):
  keep the English term. Explain it in Kirundi only if the English message explains it.

## Dates, days, times, numbers

You do not type these. Each one is a blank such as `{2}` that the checker fills in. Write the
words around the blank so the result reads naturally.

| Blank for | Is filled in as | Example of wording around it |
|---|---|---|
| a date | `20 Munyonyo 2026` (day, month name, year) | `ku itariki {2}` |
| a month | `Kigarama`, or `Ruheshi 2027` | `mu kwezi kwa {1}` |
| a weekday | `ku wa gatanu` (with a capital at the start of a sentence) | `{1}` alone; it already includes "ku" |
| a time | `15:15` (24-hour), or the digits as written when the message has no a.m./p.m. | `isaha {3}` |
| a copied detail | exactly the text of the message | `Muhamagare {4}` |

The weekday blank already contains "ku". For "every Wednesday" write `buri ndwi {1}`; for "by
Friday" write `bitarenze {1}`.

Month names the checker writes: Nzero, Ruhuhuma, Ntwarante, Ndamukiza, Rusama, Ruheshi,
Mukakaro, Myandagaro, Nyakanga, Gitugutu, Munyonyo, Kigarama. Weekdays: ku wa mbere, ku wa
kabiri, ku wa gatatu, ku wa kane, ku wa gatanu, ku wa gatandatu, ku w'indwi.

A small number that the message wrote as a word ("two forms") may be written as a Kirundi word.
Never write a clock time, a date or a month number in words: Kirundi counts the hours of the day
differently from English, so a time in words is easy to get wrong. Use the blank.

Sources for the names, read on 2026-11-14: Unicode CLDR Kirundi (rn) locale, the BCU Kirundi
dictionary (matana.de), and Zorc and Nibagwire, *Kinyarwanda and Kirundi Comparative Grammar*
(2007).

## Kirundi, not Kinyarwanda

Kinyarwanda is a close neighbour and easy to slip into. Write Kirundi:

- Kirundi spells `c`, `j`, `sh`, `vy` where Kinyarwanda spells `cy`, `jy`, `shy`, `by`:
  `cane` not `cyane`, `jewe` not `jyewe`, `gushira` not `gushyira`, `umuvyeyi` not `umubyeyi`.
- "To have" is `-fise` in Kirundi (`mfise`, `mufise`), not `-fite`.
- The future marker is `-zo-` in Kirundi (`azokora`), not `-za-`.
- Month names differ completely. Use only the table above. Kinyarwanda `Nyakanga` means July,
  but in Kirundi `Nyakanga` is September.

The checker rejects the spellings in the first two lines. The rest is yours to get right.

## What this profile cannot promise

No Kirundi speaker has reviewed this profile or any translation made with it. Treat every result
as a careful draft until a bilingual person has read it.
